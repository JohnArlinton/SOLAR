Ecosystem overview

Two routes, one energy core. Route A — property ownership (opportunistic): buy the commercial property and control roof, building, and tenancy. Route B — rooftop lease/licence (primary): secure 20–30 year roof rights while the SPV owns the PV and BESS; lower capital, scalable portfolio. Both routes run PV plus optional BESS sized from load profile and export limit. Rooftop PV feeds the building and the grid through inverters, LV switchboard, and transformer; a ground-mounted BESS compound with an EMS dispatches stored energy. The Tier-1 tenant onsite PPA is the anchor buyer; surplus exports are bought by a licensed retailer or wholesale market participant. The aggregator-VPP controls and optimises the fleet and monetises flexibility — it never buys energy. The DNSP and VicGrid provide connection and transport only — they never buy energy. AEMO/NEM is the market and settlement framework, not a customer.

Steps

#StepDetail
1Pick the routeRoute A buys the property (roof + building + tenancy; high capital, slow scale). Route B, the primary, secures 20–30 year roof rights while the SPV owns PV + BESS (lower capital, scalable portfolio). Hybrid: SolarCo holds owned + leased assets as one energy portfolio.
2Secure roof rightsRoute A: ownership. Route B: SPV rooftop lease/licence with landlord; tripartite terms bind the tenant.
3Serve onsite load firstPV output offsets high daytime tenant load through the LV switchboard; the tenant onsite PPA is the anchor offtake.
4Convert and step upInverters (AS/NZS 4777) feed LV; transformer steps up toward the DNSP connection. DNSP and VicGrid transport energy; they never buy it.
5Store and dispatchGround-mounted BESS with EMS charges from surplus and discharges at value; BESS size comes from load profile + export limit, never a generic ratio.
6Meter and settleMeter splits onsite use from export; the tenant pays the PPA for onsite volumes, a licensed retailer or wholesale participant buys export and settles via the NEM (under-30 MW General Exemption Order pathway). The aggregator-VPP optimises the fleet for flexibility value but never buys.
7Report to communityEngagement and social-value reporting runs alongside operations.

Risks

Roof replacement mid-term. A short roof life strands the asset. Verify structural + remaining life before close.
Weak DNSP connection. Limited export or costly augmentation. Confirm capacity at enquiry, not at build. The DNSP is transport only — never count it as a buyer.
Tenant churn. A 5-year lease cannot carry a 20–30 year asset. Lock long, bankable offtake (or re-letting cover) before close.
