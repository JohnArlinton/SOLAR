Dispatch and settlement

Energy flows PV → onsite tenant load first under the anchor PPA; surplus charges the BESS or exports. The EMS dispatches discharge at value. Export is bought by a licensed retailer or wholesale market participant under the under-30 MW General Exemption Order pathway and settles through AEMO/NEM; the aggregator-VPP monetises flexibility (arbitrage, FCAS) but never buys energy. The DNSP and VicGrid carry the energy — they never buy it. Revenue stack: onsite savings (primary), export (secondary), arbitrage (secondary), FCAS (upside), certificates (haircut). Worked illustration only: 60/40 onsite/export at 0.12/0.06 — a planning assumption, not a tariff.

Steps

#FlowDetail
1Onsite firstEvery PV kWh offsets Tier-1 tenant load under the anchor PPA before anything else; highest value per the stack.
2Charge surplusMidday surplus charges the BESS instead of exporting at weak prices.
3Discharge at valueEMS discharges into evening load or priced export windows; the aggregator-VPP optimises timing for arbitrage and FCAS but never buys the energy.
4Export remainderOnly genuine surplus exports via the licensed retailer or wholesale participant (<30 MW exempt pathway); AEMO negative-price periods can punish excess. The retailer is not obliged to buy at our price.
5SettleTenant pays the onsite PPA; the retailer/wholesale participant settles NEM export; FCAS is upside, certificates take a haircut. DNSP/VicGrid settle nothing — transport only.

Risks

Illustration mistaken for offer. The 60/40 at 0.12/0.06 split is an example, not a bankable tariff. Re-price per site.
Negative prices. Export-heavy dispatch can earn little or less. Keep onsite-first logic.
Aggregator mistaken for buyer. The aggregator-VPP monetises flexibility; only the licensed retailer or wholesale participant buys export. Contract accordingly.
