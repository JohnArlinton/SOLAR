Business routes · flows 01, 02, 07

Two routes, one energy core. Route A (property ownership, opportunistic): buy the commercial property and control roof, building, and tenancy — high capital, slow scale, property + energy asset. Route B (rooftop lease/licence, primary): 20–30 year roof rights while the SPV owns PV + BESS — much lower capital, scalable portfolio. Hybrid: SolarCo holds owned + leased assets as one energy portfolio. Preliminary winner: Route B.Source: HEJ business model realignment, Sep 2026 · confirm capital and term per site
Same energy core both routes: PV + optional BESS (sized from load profile + export limit, never a generic ratio) + tenant offtake + grid export.Source: sizing rule · verify load and offer per site

Market roles: who buys, who carries, who optimises · flows 01, 03, 06

Anchor buyer: the Tier-1 tenant under an onsite PPA. Export buyer: a licensed retailer or wholesale market participant. Under-30 MW General Exemption Order pathway: export sold to a licensed retailer.Source: offtake model · re-price per site
Never buyers: the aggregator/VPP (controls and optimises the fleet, monetises flexibility via arbitrage and FCAS — it does not buy energy); the DNSP (AusNet, CitiPower, Jemena, Powercor, United Energy) provides connection, transport, and constraints only; VicGrid does transmission planning and connections only and rarely touches sub-2 MW rooftop; AEMO/NEM is the market and settlement framework, not a customer.Source: regulatory map · confirm offer per site

Investment gates and offtake strategy · flow 02

Five investment gates for every site: (1) roof control, (2) tenant load/offtake, (3) DNSP connection/export, (4) approvals (planning/electrical/fire/BESS per address), (5) bankable offtake + economics. Due diligence runs as parallel property/tenant/energy tracks converging in the financial model, then a GO/NO-GO gate, then financial close → construction → operation → replicate (Route B: Warehouse 001 → 002 → …).Source: screening model · verify per site
Explicit Energy Offtake Strategy step before financial close: who pays per kWh, what contract/price/term, under what connection and market rules.Source: offtake model · lock at close

Route cautions · flows 02, 07

Tenant churn risk; a 5-year lease is insufficient for a 20–30 year asset; mortgagee consent required; roof repair/replacement duties defined upfront; connection cost unknowns held as walk-away thresholds; the retailer is not obliged to buy at our price.Source: risk register · price and assign every row before close

Ecosystem · flows 01, 06

Thesis: a distributed energy platform, not a solar farm on a roof. First pilot 0.5–2 MWp PV with 1–4 MWh BESS on a long-lived roof with high daytime load and a strong DNSP connection.Source: research Ch.1 + Ch.11 pilot blueprint · planning assumption
Chain: roof rights → structure → load → connection → PV + BESS → dispatch → offtake → settlement → O&M → exit.Source: research bottom line · sequence reference

Site screening · flow 02

Parallel property/tenant/energy tracks with a scoring matrix and 8 data requests; structural and DNSP evidence converge before commercial spend. See also investment gates.Source: research Ch.3 · verify request list at site

Planning thresholds · flows 02, 03

Planning permit path at or above 1 MW goes to the minister; ESC licensing, AEMO/NEM registration, and ESV standards apply by size and role.Source: research Ch.2 regulatory map · planning assumption, confirm with adviser

Grid connection · flow 03

DNSP-specific pathway (Powercor, CitiPower, Jemena, United Energy): enquiry → application → offer → capacity/augmentation → approval → energisation.Source: research Ch.2–Ch.4 · confirm offer per site

Yield and footprint · flows 02, 04

About 1 MWp yields ~1.31 GWh/yr in Melbourne (~3.6 kWh/kW/day); about 6000–8000 m² of roof per MWp.Source: research Ch.4 · planning assumptions, verify with SunSPOT + shading

Battery · flows 04, 07

Usable capacity sits below nameplate; ground-mounted compound preferred; thermal and fire design throughout; augmentation budget at about years 7–10.Source: research Ch.5 · planning assumption

Install and commission · flow 05

ESV standards AS/NZS 5033, 5139, 4777; CFA fire requirements; EPA noise and waste scope; DNSP witness before energisation.Source: research Ch.2 · obligations, not design values

Revenue stack · flow 06

Onsite savings primary; export secondary; arbitrage secondary; FCAS upside; certificates at a haircut. Worked illustration 60/40 onsite/export at 0.12/0.06 is an example, not a tariff; AEMO negative-price periods punish export-heavy dispatch.Source: research Ch.1 + Ch.8 · illustration only

Offtake · flow 06

Onsite tenant PPA as anchor vs export bought by a licensed retailer or wholesale participant; aggregator-VPP monetises flexibility only; settlement follows metering of onsite vs export volumes. Retailer names in the research are examples; offers change. See also market roles and market contacts.Source: research Ch.6 · re-price per site

Leases · flows 01, 07

SPV rooftop licence plus tripartite terms; 14-clause list; landlord payment for roof use; make-good and exit conditions defined upfront.Source: research Ch.7 · confirm with legal adviser

EPC and vendors · flows 04, 05

LONGi/Jinko/Sungrow as panel/inverter examples (not endorsements); 8 EPC essentials price the build; CER STC treatment for 100 kW–1 MW applies from Oct 2026.Source: research Ch.9 + Ch.2 · verify dates and specs
